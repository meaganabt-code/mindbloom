Explore and practise

10 strategies for student learning

Filter by intelligence dimension, try the activity, then mark it complete when you have practised it.

1. Daily Brain-Training Activities

IQ

Develop reasoning, memory, concentration, and problem-solving through regular cognitive activities.

Try it: Spend 15–20 minutes on Sudoku, a logic puzzle, a math problem, or a strategy game. Then summarize a lesson in your own words.

2. Daily Emotional Journaling

EQ

Recognize emotions, identify possible triggers, and reflect on responses to everyday experiences.

Try it: Write about one experience today. Ask: “What did I feel? What caused it? How could I respond differently next time?”

3. Sensory Observation Exercises

ESQ

Notice sensory information and reflect on how the environment may relate to emotions and attention.

Try it: Spend 5–10 minutes noticing safe, everyday details such as sounds, colors, textures, and smells. Record three observations and your reactions.

4. Mindful Learning Activities

IQ · EQ · ESQ

Pay attention to thoughts, emotions, bodily sensations, and surroundings before, during, and after learning.

Try it: Before studying, spend 5 minutes focusing on comfortable breathing. Notice distractions without judging yourself, then return to the task. Reflect on what helped your focus.

5. Creative Arts and Sensory Expression

EQ · ESQ

Explore emotions and sensory experiences through drawing, painting, music, or creative writing.

Try it: Create a drawing or short piece of writing representing a feeling after a challenging school day. Reflect on the colors, shapes, sounds, or words you chose.

6. Reading Comprehension and Analytical Reading

IQ

Build understanding, vocabulary, reasoning, and information-evaluation skills through careful reading.

Try it: Read an article or lesson. Identify the main idea and supporting evidence, summarize the argument, and write one conclusion based on the information.

7. Peer Discussion and Active Listening

EQ

Practise empathy, respectful communication, and understanding different perspectives during group work.

Try it: Let a classmate finish speaking, summarize what you heard, and ask a respectful question before sharing your perspective.

8. Environmental Awareness and Sensory Regulation

ESQ

Observe how lighting, temperature, background noise, movement, and study-space arrangements affect comfort and concentration.

Try it: Record one environmental factor that helped and one that distracted you. If possible, make one safe adjustment for your next study session.

9. Visual Observation and Emotional Interpretation

ESQ

Examine visual details and reflect on how images or surroundings may prompt different feelings and memories.

Try it: Observe two photographs or landscapes. Note their colors, shapes, and patterns, then write down feelings or memories associated with each image.

10. Sensory Journaling and Emotional Tracking

ESQ

Reflect on connections between sensory experiences and emotional responses through structured journaling.

Try it: Record one sensory experience, the emotion you noticed, and a possible explanation. Example: “Loud noise — distracted — difficult to focus on reading.”
